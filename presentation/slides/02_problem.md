Slide Number: 2
Slide Title: Microplastic pollution is difficult to track
Purpose: Establish the environmental problem and motivation for the study.
Main Message: Microplastic pollution is pervasive, monitoring is spatially sparse, and river networks connect upstream pollution to downstream areas.
Exact Text/Copy:
Microplastic pollution is difficult to track because:
• Observations are spatially sparse and expensive to collect
• Concentrations vary across locations and time
• River systems are connected networks: pollution upstream can influence downstream areas
• Traditional monitoring requires extensive in-situ sampling
Recommended Visual: A river-system diagram illustrating transport:
[Urban area] → [Stormwater] → [Upstream river] → [Monitoring station] → [Downstream river] → [Ocean]
Highlight how contamination can move through connected waterways.
Figure/Table Requirement: Create a simple diagram (can be hand-drawn or schematic) showing the flow of microplastics from urban sources through the river network to the ocean, with monitoring points along the way.
Speaker Notes:
- 30-second explanation: "Microplastics enter rivers from sources like urban runoff and can travel downstream, making monitoring challenging."
- 60–90-second explanation: "Microplastic pollution originates from various sources, including urban stormwater and wastewater discharges. Once in the river, plastics can be transported downstream by water currents, meaning that pollution observed at one location may have originated far upstream. Traditional monitoring relies on collecting water samples at specific points, which is labor-intensive and cannot capture the full spatial and temporal variability. Because rivers are interconnected networks, understanding transport requires considering how pollution moves from upstream to downstream areas."
- Technical explanation: "The advective transport of microplastics in rivers is governed by water flow velocity, turbulence, and particle settling velocities. In sparse sampling regimes, inferring transport patterns from point measurements alone is challenging without explicit modeling of hydrological connectivity."
- One-sentence explanation: "Microplastics move with river currents, so pollution upstream can affect downstream areas."
Transition to Next Slide: "Existing prediction methods often miss this connectivity—let's examine the knowledge gap."
